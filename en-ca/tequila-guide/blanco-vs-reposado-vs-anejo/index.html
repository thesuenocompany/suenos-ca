Blanco vs. reposado vs. añejo

Blanco puts fresh agave up front. Reposado adds a layer of oak without losing that agave character. Añejo spends longer in wood and usually brings deeper barrel notes. Choose by how you want it to taste, not by how long it aged.

Sueños Tequila

StyleAgeingWhat to expectTry it in
BlancoUsually unagedFresh agave, citrus, pepperA crisp Margarita, Paloma or Ranch Water
ReposadoAt least 2 months in oakAgave with gentle oak, sometimes vanilla or spiceA rounder Margarita or a pour on its own
AñejoAt least 1 year in oakDeeper oak notes, often caramel, spice or dried fruitSipping or a tequila Old Fashioned

Those are the minimums set by Mexico’s Tequila Regulatory Council (CRT). The barrel and the producer can change the flavour; age alone isn’t a quality score.

Choose by the drink

For a Margarita with a bright lime snap, start with blanco. If you want the same drink a little rounder, swap in reposado and keep the rest of the recipe unchanged. For sipping, taste them side by side before deciding. If you love an oak-forward spirit, añejo is a good place to start.

Does darker mean better?

No. Colour tells you less about flavour than you might think. Don’t use “gold” as a synonym for reposado: the Tequila Regulatory Council (CRT) lists joven/oro as a separate class. Read the class on the label instead.
